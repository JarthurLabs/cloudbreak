I built a little cloud security game where blocking everything is a good way to lose.

Meet Cloudbreak.

Play Cloudbreak in your browser: https://cloudbreak.onrender.com/

You have three minutes to protect a futuristic city from bad logins, request swarms, and heavier attacks with working keys. The buildings represent cloud services. The catch? Real customers need to get through too.

Check credentials when the keys are wrong. Limit traffic when the requests come too quickly. Isolate a route when you need to stop every arrival, then reopen it when the danger clears.

The city can survive an attack and still fail if too many customers are turned away.

I built this with Codex. I directed the gameplay, visuals, onboarding, balance, and sound, while Codex handled implementation and testing.

A lot of that work started with something feeling wrong during a playthrough. Customer traffic looked like dice. The tutorial took too many clicks. Gates felt slow. Turning on a defense appeared to reset the approaching enemies. And the first music options sounded far too relaxed for a city under attack.

Apparently the security team needed a more urgent soundtrack.

Those reviews became specific changes to the controls, request flow, damage feedback, and city design. I chose Firewall Drive for the music.

The project connects with my experience in software-as-a-service implementation, documentation, onboarding, and troubleshooting. A setting can look right on its own and still interrupt the workflow it is supposed to support. I wanted that consequence to be part of the game.

Underneath the city, synthetic requests go through a real HTTP gateway inside the game. The response codes drive the results. It is a teaching model, not a production cloud environment.

The 4K video shows the decisions in action. The GitHub write-up covers how we built it, including the parts that needed another pass.
